## 0. THE GOVERNING IDEA: THE VOICE HAS TWO WEIGHTS

The logo system is one heavy mark and one thin one. The OT monogram is loud, angular, athletic. The OUR TIME wordmark is quiet, light, wide. That contrast is the brand's signature, and the writing has to reproduce it rather than invent a third voice.

So the copy has two weights and only two:

**HEAVY.** Display lines. Uppercase. Fragments. Full stops. Three to five words. Printed on garments, set as hero type, used as email subjects. `EARN EVERY SECOND.`

**LIGHT.** Working copy. Sentence case. Plain, specific, unhurried. Product descriptions, emails, care instructions, policy pages. *"320gsm cotton. It softens from about the fifth wash and it will not go back."*

Everything you will ever write is one of those two. If a sentence is trying to be both — a product description that shouts, a display line that explains — it is wrong. Set it in the other weight and cut it again.

**Never shout twice.** If the monogram is on the page, the words go quiet. The typography carries the volume; the copy does not have to.

## 1. TONE OF VOICE

Four principles. Each has one rule. Each has a DO and a DON'T written in the brand's actual voice.

---

### 1.1 SAY IT IN ONE BREATH.

**Rule:** Display lines are one breath long. Fragments are allowed. Full stops are compulsory. Commas usually mean you have not finished cutting.

> **DO** — `TIME WON'T WAIT.`
> **DON'T** — `Time waits for no one, so make sure you're making the most of every moment!`

The full stop on a fragment is the whole trick. It turns a phrase into a statement of fact. `OUR TIME.` is a claim. `Our time` is a caption. Keep the stop.

---

### 1.2 STATE, DON'T SELL.

**Rule:** Say what the thing is, what it is made of, and when it goes. Never say it is special. Specification is the persuasion.

> **DO** — `380gsm loopback. Boxy. Garment-dyed in small lots, so no two run the same.`
> **DON'T** — `Elevate your everyday with this must-have premium essential.`

No superlatives. No "iconic", no "luxury", no "premium" in customer-facing copy. If the product is premium, the weight, the fit line and the photograph say so. Adjectives about quality are what you write when you have no facts.

---

### 1.3 THE WORK, NOT THE WINNING.

**Rule:** The brand is about hours put in, not rewards taken out. It respects the person doing the shift. It never coaches them, never shames them, never sells them a lifestyle.

> **DO** — `NOBODY HANDS YOU THE HOUR.`
> **DON'T** — `RISE AND GRIND. NO DAYS OFF. 💪`

This is the line that keeps OUR TIME out of the grind-culture bin, and it is worth being blunt about: grind-culture language is not an aesthetic problem, it is a positioning problem. Every label with a clock on it sounds like that. The difference between OUR TIME and a thousand dropshipped motivation brands is that we describe work honestly — including rest, weather, dark mornings and the fact that some hours are just hours — instead of performing it. Discipline is quiet. Write it quiet.

Corollary: never use the second person as an instruction. `SHOW UP.` is a command from a stranger. `THE HOURS SHOW.` is an observation. The second one is ours.

---

### 1.4 PLACE OVER POSTURE.

**Rule:** When you need texture, reach for the real place — the Black Isle, the firths, the light, the weather — not for attitude.

> **DO** — `MADE FOR LONG DAYS. AND THE SHORT ONES.`
> **DON'T** — `BUILT DIFFERENT. BUILT FOR THE STREETS.`

The coordinates on the globe tee are 57.5878° N, 4.2383° W. That is a peninsula in the Scottish Highlands with sea on both sides, farmland down the middle, dolphins off Chanonry Point, eighteen-hour days in June and about six in December. A streetwear label from there is genuinely rare. A streetwear label about grinding is not. Use the place.

Two guardrails, because this is where it could go badly wrong:

- **No tartan-shortbread.** No "wee", no "bonnie", no Gaelic words used decoratively, no clan language, no thistles, no Nessie. The Highlands here are weather and working hours, not heritage tourism.
- **Never claim manufacture you do not have.** `STARTED ON THE BLACK ISLE.` is true and sayable. "Made in Scotland" is a legal claim. Do not write it unless it is.

---

### 1.5 MECHANICS (the boring rules that hold the four above together)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Exclamation marks | Never. Not once. Not in emails, not in DMs, not in a reply to a customer. |
| Emoji | Never, in any brand-owned channel. |
| Uppercase | Display only. Never a full sentence of body copy in caps. |
| Ampersand | Avoid. Write "and". The `&` glyph fights the wordmark. |
| Oxford comma | Use it. `DISCIPLINE. FOCUS. CONSISTENCY.` is stops, not commas, but where commas appear, be consistent. |
| Ellipsis | Never. It is hesitation. |
| Numerals | Digits, always. "24", "0600", "2026". Never "twenty-four". |
| Time format | 24-hour, four digits, leading zero, no colon: `0600`, not `6:00am`. |
| Dates | `16.09.26` in stamps, `16 September 2026` in prose. Never American order. |
| Currency | `£45`. No decimals on whole amounts. |
| Voice | Active. "We ship Thursday," not "orders will be shipped." |
| Person | "We" for the brand. "You" for the customer, sparingly, never as a command. |
| Contractions | Yes, in LIGHT copy. No, in HEAVY copy — display lines are too short to need them, except where the line already exists (`TIME WON'T WAIT.`). |

## 2. THE LEXICON

### 2.1 THE WORD LIST — words the brand owns

Pull from these. They are all concrete, all either time or place, none of them aspirational.

**Time, plain:** second · minute · hour · day · week · morning · night · early · late · long · short · half past · quarter

**Time, working:** shift · clock on · clock off · the early · the back shift · the night · rota · overtime *(see the ban below — the concept is allowed, the word is not)* · break · start · finish · hours · logged

**Horology:** watch · dial · face · hand · mark · index · crown · wind · set · keep time · running · stopped · movement · tick · second hand

**Black Isle and weather:** light · first light · last light · dawn · dusk · gloaming · dark · firth · tide · ebb · flood · slack water · turn · shore · field · haar · wind · rain · frost · summer light · winter dark

**Cloth:** gsm · loopback · jersey · twill · piqué · garment-dyed · washed · overdyed · boxy · dropped shoulder · relaxed · fade · soften · shrink · wear in · seam · cuff · hem

**Verbs the brand likes:** earn · keep · hold · carry · wear · start · finish · put in · last · fade · turn · wait · count · log · run

**Numbers with meaning:** 24 / 7 (artwork mark only) · EST. 2026 · 57.5878° N · 4.2383° W · the hour codes (Section 4)

---

### 2.2 THE BAN LIST — words the brand never uses

**Hard ban. No exceptions, no irony, no "but it works here".**

`hustle` · `grind` · `grind-set` · `grindset` · `no days off` · `rise and grind` · `sleep is for the weak` · `elevate your` · `level up` · `unlock` · `empower` · `curated` · `drip` · `fit check` · `heat` · `fire` · `iconic` · `timeless` · `game-changer` · `must-have` · `essentials` · `journey` · `vibes` · `aesthetic` (as a noun) · `luxury` · `exclusive` · `limited` (unless the number is stated and true) · `just dropped` · `restock alert` · `back by popular demand` · `link in bio` · emoji · exclamation marks

**Three of those deserve their reason on the record:**

- **`timeless`** — a time brand calling its clothes timeless is both the most obvious joke available and a lie about a garment that is designed to fade. We sell things that visibly record time passing. Say that instead: `IT FADES BECAUSE YOU WORE IT.`
- **`limited`** — scarcity you assert is marketing; scarcity you can count is a fact. If there are 60, write `60 made.` If you do not know, say nothing.
- **`curated`** — nobody curated anything. You picked some colours.

**Special case — `OVERTIME`.**
This word is banned outright, in every context: copy, captions, print, file names, internal chat, jokes. The brand is OUR TIME. Garments have already gone to production reading OVERTIME. The word is now radioactive; treating it as a near-miss synonym is exactly how the misprint happened. The working concept is available through other language — `the back shift`, `the late one`, `hours nobody paid for` — and those are better lines anyway.

**Special case — `24 / 7`.**
It is a mark, not a sentence. It is permitted flanking the orbital globe in artwork, as it already appears. It is not permitted in body copy, subject lines or social captions, where it reads as exactly the grind-culture cliché Principle 1.3 exists to prevent.

**Special case — countdowns and urgency.**
Do not write `ending soon`, `last chance`, `hurry`, `don't miss out`, or anything that implies a timer. An always-on countdown was already killed at the site level and the same logic binds the copy: manufactured urgency from a brand about time is the cheapest possible move and everyone can see it.

### 2.3 THE HOUSE STYLE FOR THE BRAND NAME

- In running text: **OUR TIME**, uppercase, two words. Not "Our Time", not "OurTime".
- Possessive: "OUR TIME's first drop" is acceptable but ugly. Rewrite: "the first OUR TIME drop".
- The monogram is called **the OT monogram**, never "the logo" in production briefs — because the wordmark is also the logo. Precision here prevents the printer sending the wrong file.
- The two marks together are **the lockup**.

## 3. COPY ARCHITECTURE

Every piece of writing the brand produces has a fixed shape and a fixed maximum length. The counts are not decoration — they are what stops a founder writing four paragraphs at midnight. Count characters including spaces and punctuation. If it does not fit, cut a word; do not shrink the type.

---

### 3.1 PRODUCT NAME

**Pattern:** `[HOUR] [GARMENT]` and, where the piece carries a back print, ` / [PRINT]`
**Maximum:** 22 characters.
**Register:** HEAVY. Uppercase. No adjectives, ever — no "oversized", no "heavyweight", no "premium". Those live in the fit line.

```
0600 CREW
1200 TEE / GLOBE
0300 JEAN / PATTERN
```

Colour is a separate field in the shop, never part of the name: `0600 CREW — Cream`.

Full system in Section 4.

---

### 3.2 PRODUCT DESCRIPTION

**Pattern:** two lines. Line one says what it is. Line two says what it is for, or what its hour means.
**Maximum:** line one 60 characters, line two 90 characters, 150 total.
**Register:** LIGHT.

```
0600 CREW — Cream
Heavy loopback crewneck in the colour of sand at first light.
Cut boxy so it sits over a tee. The hour you get up, not the hour you're due.
```

```
1600 SHORT — Washed Denim
Washed slate sweat short, the colour of the firth mid-afternoon.
Drawcord waist, deep pockets, cut above the knee. Built to be worn wet.
```

```
2200 TEE / HOURGLASS — Navy
Oversized heavyweight tee with a distressed hourglass across the back.
Navy deep enough to read as black indoors. The last useful hour of the day.
```

Banned inside a description: any sentence beginning "Perfect for", "Whether you're", or "Made with love".

---

### 3.3 THE FABRIC / FIT LINE

One line, sitting directly under the description. This is the single most persuasive sentence on the page, and it contains no persuasion.

**Pattern:** `[weight and fibre]. [fit]. [finish and behaviour].`
**Maximum:** 80 characters.
**Register:** LIGHT, clipped to the point of being a spec.

```
380gsm loopback cotton. Boxy, dropped shoulder. Garment-dyed, fades with wear.
240gsm piqué cotton. Regular, ribbed collar. Holds its shape after washing.
14oz rigid denim. Baggy, straight leg. Stiff for a month, then it's yours.
```

Rule: if you do not know the gsm, ask the factory before you write the line. Do not guess and do not write "heavyweight" as a substitute for a number. A number is the brand; an adjective is every other brand.

---

### 3.4 DROP ANNOUNCEMENT

Three parts, always in this order.

**Stamp** — the date and time in brand format. `16.09.26 / 1900`
**Headline** — HEAVY. Maximum 34 characters.
**Body** — LIGHT. Maximum 120 characters. Says what is in it and how many.
**Call to action** — maximum 14 characters. Verb first.

```
16.09.26 / 1900

THE FIRST HOURS.

Seven tees, five polos, four crews, four hoodies. Washed and garment-dyed.
Nothing here is restocked.

SEE THE INDEX
```

The CTA vocabulary is fixed and short: `SEE THE INDEX` · `VIEW THE DROP` · `BUY` · `SET A REMINDER`. Never `SHOP NOW`.

---

### 3.5 SOLD-OUT LINE

**Maximum:** 30 characters. Past tense. No apology, no consolation, no notify-me badgering.

```
GONE.
THAT HOUR'S PASSED.
ALL 60 GONE.
SOLD OUT. NOT RETURNING.
```

If a restock genuinely is coming, say the date or say nothing:
```
BACK 04.11.26.
```

Never: "Sold out 😭", "Notify me when back in stock!", "You snooze you lose".

---

### 3.6 EMAIL SUBJECT LINES

**Maximum:** 38 characters (mobile truncates near 40). No emoji. No exclamation. No "RE:" tricks. No first name merge tags — the brand does not do false intimacy.
**Preheader:** maximum 60 characters, LIGHT, and it must add information, never repeat the subject.

| Subject (HEAVY) | Preheader (LIGHT) |
|---|---|
| `THE FIRST HOURS. 16.09.26 / 1900.` | `Twenty-eight pieces. Seven garments. One drop.` |
| `0600 IS BACK IN CREAM.` | `Four sizes only. We made sixty.` |
| `YOUR ORDER LEAVES THURSDAY.` | `Tracking follows when it's scanned, not before.` |
| `THE BLACK ISLE, 57.5878° N.` | `Why the coordinates on the globe tee changed.` |
| `NOTHING TO SELL YOU THIS WEEK.` | `A note on how the garments are dyed.` |

That last one is permitted and encouraged. A brand that only emails to sell is a shop; a brand that occasionally emails with nothing to sell is a brand.

---

### 3.7 CARE LABEL

The legal content — fibre composition, country of origin, the standard care symbols — is set by regulation and by your manufacturer. It goes on the label first and it is not ours to write. What follows is the voice line printed underneath it.

**Pattern:** five lines, imperative, LIGHT, maximum 32 characters per line.

```
WASH COLD. INSIDE OUT.
HANG DRY.
IRON LOW. NOT ON THE PRINT.
HOT WATER WILL TAKE THE COLOUR.
IT WILL FADE. THAT IS THE POINT.
```

That final line is the brand's whole philosophy sitting inside a garment where only the owner will find it. Keep it on every washed or garment-dyed piece. Omit it on the polos, where fading is not the intent.

**Front:** the lockup only. OT monogram above, OUR TIME wordmark below, `EST. 2026` beneath at the wordmark's tracking. No price, no barcode, no web address on this face.

in small lots. No two run the same.

**Maximum:** the descriptive line is 70 characters over two lines. The coordinates and EST. line never change.

### 3.9 THE 404

**Heading:** HEAVY, maximum 24 characters.
**Body:** LIGHT, maximum 90 characters.
**Link:** maximum 16 characters.

```
THIS HOUR IS EMPTY.

The page has gone, or it never existed. Everything live is on the index.

THE INDEX
```

Do not put a joke here. Do not put an hourglass here. Do not animate anything here.

### 3.10 ORDER CONFIRMATION EMAIL

The tone problem with confirmation emails is gratitude inflation. Ours logs the transaction like a ledger entry and then tells the truth about timing.

**Subject:** `ORDER 1042. LOGGED.` (maximum 24 characters)
**Preheader:** `Leaves Thursday. Tracking when it's scanned.`

```
ORDER 1042. LOGGED.

We have it.

1 × 0600 CREW — Cream, L          £85
1 × 1600 SHORT — Washed Denim, M  £55
                        Total     £140

It leaves on Thursday 18.09. You'll get tracking when it's
scanned by the courier, not before — a tracking number that

Wrong size, wrong colour, wrong decision: 30 days, unworn,
tags on. Reply to this email and we'll sort it.

OUR TIME
57.5878° N  4.2383° W
```

Sign-off is the wordmark and the coordinates. Never "Thanks again!", never "Team OUR TIME", never "Happy shopping".

**Shipping email subject:** `ORDER 1042 IS MOVING.`
**Delivered email:** do not send one. The courier already did.

## 4. THE PRODUCT NAMING SYSTEM

This is the part the brand most urgently does not have. Seven garment types and twenty-eight colourways are currently identified only by description, which is why production has already gone wrong once.

### 4.1 SYSTEMS CONSIDERED, AND WHY THEY LOSE

**Horology terms** — *Escapement, Mainspring, Balance, Crown, Bezel.*
Beautiful vocabulary, and genuinely on-brand. Rejected for three reasons: it runs dry at about fifteen usable words, so drop three is naming things "Hairspring"; it is unassignable by a non-specialist, because nothing about a cream hoodie tells you whether it is a Mainspring or a Bezel; and it is the exact register where "premium minimal" tips into "watch-forum twee".

**Tides** — *Flood, Ebb, Slack, Springs, Neaps.*
Black Isle, true, tactile. Rejected: four states, which is not a system, it is four names. Also tidal language is already heavily worked by coastal surf brands.

**Highland light / Scots words** — *Gloaming, Simmer Dim, Haar.*
Evocative and differentiated. Rejected as a primary system for the reason set out in Principle 1.4: a full range named in dialect Scots reads as heritage tourism, and a non-Scottish customer cannot pronounce, spell or search it. These words are excellent *inside* copy. They are poor as SKUs.

**Shift patterns** — *Early, Back, Night.*
Honest and on-theme, but only three terms, and "Night Hoodie" sounds like sleepwear.

**A bare numbered index** — *001, 002, 003.*
Scales infinitely, survives any founder, never twee. Rejected alone because the numbers carry no meaning — 014 tells you nothing, so nobody remembers it and it does no work for the brand.

### 4.2 THE SYSTEM: THE 24-HOUR INDEX

**Every colour in the brand is permanently assigned an hour of the day, chosen because the colour looks like that hour on the Black Isle. The product name is that hour plus the garment type.**

```
[HHMM] [GARMENT]            0600 CREW
[HHMM] [GARMENT] / [PRINT]  1200 TEE / GLOBE
```

It wins on all five tests:

1. **Time-derived and place-derived at once.** The hours are a clock; the reason each colour holds its hour is Highland light. Both halves of the brand, one field.
2. **It scales.** 24 hours × 7 garment types = 168 name slots before you touch half-hours, and half-hours (`0230`) are there if you ever need them.
3. **It is unsupervisable-proof.** The founder never invents a name. He picks a colour; the colour already owns an hour; the hour is in a locked table. Naming becomes lookup, not judgement.
4. **It cannot go twee.** Numbers do not emote. This is the system's single greatest safety feature, given a brand one bad decision away from "The Gloaming Hoodie".
5. **It merchandises.** Because the hour belongs to the colour, not the garment, a shared hour makes a set. Everything at 0300 is washed black, head to toe. "The 0300 set" sells itself, and the founder did not have to think of it.

**Writing the hour:** four digits, leading zero, no colon, no am/pm. `0600`, never `6:00`. The colon is deliberately withheld — it reads as a running clock and drags the brand toward the countdown cliché that has already been ruled out.

**Saying the hour:** "oh-six-hundred crew", "twenty-two-hundred tee". Use this in video, on calls and with the factory.

**On the garment:** the hour is *not* printed on the outside. It goes on the internal neck label, under the wordmark, small. Only the owner sees it. It costs nothing and it is the kind of detail that makes a label feel like a system rather than a shop.

### 4.4 GARMENT TYPE VOCABULARY

Fixed, singular, plain. No invented category names.

`TEE` · `POLO` · `CREW` · `HOODIE` · `SHORT` · `PANT` · `JEAN`

Future types slot in the same way: `CAP`, `JACKET`, `BEANIE`, `SCARF`. Never `CRUISER`, never `FOUNDATION`, never `MK.II`.

### 4.5 PRINT SUFFIX VOCABULARY

Only used when a garment carries a back or all-over graphic. One word, after a slash.

`/ WORDMARK` · `/ HOURGLASS` · `/ GLOBE` · `/ WATCH` · `/ PATTERN` · `/ DIAL`

A chest-only OT monogram is the default and takes **no suffix**. New graphics get a new one-word suffix, added to this list, and the list lives in this document.

### 4.6 SKU FORMAT

For the developer, the factory and the spreadsheet:

```
OT-[HOUR]-[TYPE]-[PRINT]-[SIZE]

OT-0600-CREW-XX-L        (no print)
OT-1200-TEE-GLB-M
OT-0300-JEAN-PAT-32
```

Print codes: `XX` none · `WDM` wordmark · `HGL` hourglass · `GLB` globe · `WCH` watch · `PAT` pattern · `DIA` dial.

### 4.7 THE FOUNDER'S THREE QUESTIONS

To name any new piece, unsupervised, in under a minute:

1. **What colour is it?** Find it in the ledger (4.3). That is your hour. If it is a genuinely new colour, ask what time of day it looks like on the Black Isle, take the nearest free hour, and add a row to the ledger. Then stop — do not adjust any existing row.
2. **What garment is it?** One word from the list in 4.4.
3. **Does it carry a back or all-over print?** If yes, add one suffix from 4.5. If it is just the OT chest mark, add nothing.

If you cannot answer question one confidently, the colour is too close to one you already own. That is a range problem, not a naming problem. Do not solve it by inventing a name.

## 6. THE RULE THAT PROTECTS THE WORDMARK

**OUR TIME is two words. Always. Everywhere. No exception has ever existed and none will be granted.**

The wordmark is light-weight, uppercase and very widely letterspaced. That construction makes the word space structurally fragile: when every letter is already separated by a large gap, the gap between words stops reading as a gap. It has already collapsed to OURTIME in production, and garments have already gone out reading OVERTIME. Both failures have the same root cause — no one owned the check.

### 6.1 THE RULE, FOR A PRINTER

1. The wordmark is supplied as **outlined vector artwork only**. No live text, no font file, ever. If you have received a font, you have received the wrong file — request the artwork again.
2. The word space measures **a minimum of 1.0 em**, measured from the right edge of the **R** to the left edge of the **T**, against a letter tracking of 0.40 em. The word space is therefore never less than **2.5× the gap between two letters**.
3. **The visual check:** you must be able to drop a capital **O** from the wordmark into the space between OUR and TIME without it touching either letter. If it touches, the space is too tight. Reject the file.
4. Never re-set, re-space, re-kern, condense, stretch or re-type the wordmark to fit a print area. Scale the whole mark proportionally or change the print area.
5. Never hyphenate, never break across two lines, never stack OUR above TIME.
6. **Read the word out loud before the screen is burned.** It says OUR TIME. If it says OURTIME or OVERTIME, stop and call the client.

### 6.2 THE RULE, FOR A DEVELOPER

Preferred implementation is a single inline SVG with the letterforms outlined — no font dependency, no reflow, no collapse.

Where live text is unavoidable (page titles, meta tags, screen-reader content), all four of these declarations are mandatory together:

```css
.wordmark {
  text-transform: uppercase;
  letter-spacing: 0.40em;
  word-spacing: 0.60em;   /* stacks with letter-spacing → ≈1.0em total gap */
  white-space: nowrap;    /* the words never break apart */
  font-weight: 300;
}
```

And in the markup, a plain space — never a non-breaking space, never two spaces, never a manually inserted `&thinsp;` or `&emsp;`:

```html
<span class="wordmark">Our Time</span>
```

Additional bindings:

- Never render the wordmark with `letter-spacing` set and `word-spacing` unset. That single omission is the exact failure mode: with tracking at 0.40em and the word space left at its default, the gap between words becomes visually indistinguishable from the gap between letters, and the mark reads OURTIME.
- Never apply `letter-spacing` to a wordmark inside a flex container without `white-space: nowrap`. At narrow widths it will wrap between the words and the mark will break.
- `<title>`, `alt` text, Open Graph tags, JSON-LD `brand.name`, the email `From` name and the store name field all read **`OUR TIME`**, two words.
- Lowercase, unspaced forms are permitted **only** where a space is technically impossible: the domain (`ourtime`), social handles (`@ourtime`), file names (`ourtime-wordmark-white.svg`), CSS class names. These are addresses, not the name.
- Trailing letter-spacing adds phantom space after the final **E**. Compensate with `margin-right: -0.40em` on the wordmark span, or centring will look wrong. This is a real bug, not a nicety.

### 6.3 THE RULE, FOR THE FOUNDER

One person owns this check, and it is you.

**The pre-press gate — nothing goes to a printer, a factory, a supplier or a marketplace until all four are ticked:**

- [ ] The artwork reads **OUR TIME**, as two words, read aloud, off the file — not off the mockup, not off memory.
- [ ] A capital O fits in the word space without touching.
- [ ] The word **OVERTIME** appears nowhere in the artwork, the file name, the order or the email thread.
- [ ] The file is outlined vector and came from the master artwork folder, not from a phone screenshot, a WhatsApp thread or a supplier's "we tidied it up" version.

**The single most important sentence in this entire document:** there is one master artwork folder and everything ships from it. The misprint did not happen because of a spacing value. It happened because there were several versions of the truth and no one had said which was the real one. Fix that and the rest of this section is a formality.
